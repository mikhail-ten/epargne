## Vue d'ensemble

[paragraphe à rédiger]

Individual investors face a search problem when buying stocks: there are thousands of possible stocks to choose from, so attention acts as a filter that first determines the choice set. Preferences then determine which stocks within that set are actually purchased. Selling does not involve the same problem because investors typically sell only stocks they already own (and most individuals do not short-sell extensively).
As a result, individual investors tend to be net buyers of “attention-grabbing” stocks. Attention is proxied by:

* Stocks in the news
* Stocks with unusually high abnormal trading volume
* Stocks with extreme one-day returns (both large positive and large negative)

It has influenced a large subsequent literature on investor attention (including work using Google search volume, media coverage, social media, and platforms like Robinhood). The findings also reinforce broader lessons from Barber and Odean’s research program: individual investors often underperform due to behavioral biases (overtrading, attention effects, the disposition effect, etc.), and a diversified buy-and-hold approach is generally preferable for most people.
In short, it is a classic, carefully executed empirical paper that cleanly demonstrates how limited attention shapes retail buying behavior in a way that is both economically meaningful and detrimental to performance.
